Imperialism is a term generally used to denote an international system of domination of certain states over others. 

Hobson (2013) defines it as 	the paternalistic belief that the duty of the imperial power is to intervene and thus uplift another group of people in order to give them a more prosperous future (19). Involves  intervention by Western countries in order to convert nations into the Western-liberal standard of civilization. 


Today, it's typically used to denote the 'informal' empire of global capitalism. 


According to Emmanuel, imperialism must be understood primarily as a theory of trade. It is about how the creation of a international division of labor leads to the systematic transfer of value from periphery to core: 

> This global analysis of capitalism must be based on economic facts, because imperialism—which is the international form of the capitalist mode of production—is first and foremost an economic phenomenon characterized by the transfer of value from exploited countries to exploiting countries. (Emmanuel, p. 76)


Another difference between imperialism as a capitalist phenomena is its relation to subordinate societies. Imperialism pre-capitalist did much less to change the internal relations of nations as does modern imperialism. Ultimately, the Roman Empire did not require much change internally, rather that the sufficient conditions to expropriate a surplus be made. This does not mean they have no effect on the internal relations of course, but rather, that capitalism is a much more corrosive social force. It truly destroys any other social formation it comes in contact with and transforms the internal relations of production to become part of global capitalism. This is the effect of capital, which evolved into full capitalism.  (see Panitch and Gindin and "global rift" book )

Imperialism is at once a political and economic category, similar to now the economic form mirrors the politicalStructure.

Features of imperialism: 
- Subsumption 
	- Creating the system
		- extra-(formal)political structure
- Dependency 
	- Control within the system
